	After presenting my WIP walking simulator to the teacher and class, I gained some useful insight and feedback on my project. These were the notes I recoreded from after my presentation:

	- insatiate + prefab of footprint + destroying the object after a certain amount of time 
	- footstep audio was on play on awake and loop, fixed that so that it works but still look into it
	- increase the volume of the footsteps

	Taking these into account, I did some research on audio code and found that if you add the following conditional statement to the line where the audio is being played whenever the player walks, it would stop if the player is not actively moving: if (_audioSource.isPlaying) { _audioSource.Stop(); }. This helped prevent the footsteps from continuing to play when the player was no longer moving and resolved the unwanted overlapping audio issue. 

	I returned to investigating the skybox issue and decided to google the implmentation process of a skybox in Unity. I discovered that the issue lied in the first-person camera all along, as the background type was set to color rather than skybox. This explained why the sky appeared to be the dark blue-grey instead of the illuminated sky that I was hoping to see. Now that those two major issues were resolved, my next task was to implement the footprints in the snow. I first started by creating a footprint prefab using a footprint image I sourced from Pinterest (https://www.pinterest.com/pin/boots-print-illustration--72198400270763368/). From there, I removed its background to make it transparent and impoted the image into Unity where I assigned it to a material using a Decal shader. I learned that using a Decal shader allows me to project a material texture directly onto the terrain, making it appear as though the player was leaving impressions in the snow. To get the decals working, I first had to enable the Decal renderer feature in the URP settings. Once that was configured, I was able to implement the footprints into the environment. 

	Finally, I modified the FP Footsteps script to incorporate an object pool for the footprints. This means that instead of continuously creating new objects, the system generates an array of 10 footprint instances and places them on the terrain in coordination with where the player goes and is facing in conjuction with the footsteps audio. Once the system notices that 10 footprints have been placed, it begins to remove the oldest footprints in place of spawning new ones. This process repeats itself until the game is reset then starts from the beginning. 

	Today was one of the more significant stages of development, as I was able to resolve several longstanding issues, such as the footprints in the snow and the skybox. In other news, fixing the footsteps audio reinforced the importance of sound in a game and how important it is to synchronize said sound with the player. I also learned that most issues usually stem from a simple configuration solution, rather than a massive overhaul of a script or asset. In the case of the skybox, the problem wasn't with the asset itself, but rather with a minor setting found in the player's camera's background settings. Additionally, implementing the footprint decals was particularly interesting, as it introduced me to the decal system in Unity as a whole, allowing me to remember and potentially use it in future projects. Overall, these additions helped bring together all of the missing pieces I had throughout the project, allowing me to reach the end and have a satisfyingly eerie snow-filled mountainous landscape that the player is free to explore. 

	As the project is now near its completion, the remaining priorities are to clean up the scripts and assets and make sure everything is functioning as intended.
