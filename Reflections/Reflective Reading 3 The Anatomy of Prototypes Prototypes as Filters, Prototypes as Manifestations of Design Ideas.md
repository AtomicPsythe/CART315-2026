	The research paper "The Anatomy of Prototypes: Prototypes as Filters, Prototypes as Manifestations of Design Ideas" written by Youn Kyung Lim, Erik Stolterman, and Josh Tenenberg redefines prototyping as an active tool for design exploration and reflection. One idea that stood out to me was that prototypes are not simply "incomplete versions of a final product", but rather a playground for experimentation. The authors explain that prototypes behave as filters, allowing designers to focus on specific aspects of a project and bring them to fruition. They also go on to highlight different aspects that can be explored through prototyping such as appearance, functionality, interactivity, materials, and resolution. 

	Another aspect that resonated with me was the authors' argument that a prototype's incompleteness can often be a project's greatest strength. I often find my stubbornness getting in the way of project development, as I strive to make multiple elements work together before advancing. This can slow down the production process and lead to frustration. However, this reading made me realize that isolating certain elements can make experimentation more manageable. For example, during my second prototyping project, I initially focused on perfecting the colliders on my opening and closing flowers before creating the second level. While it was frustrating, I learned about polygon colliders, which helped me overcome that obstacle and many similar collision-related problems later on.

	This reading also encouraged me to recognize the importance of reflecting on previous iterations when prototyping. While the final product may feel like the most successful version, looking back at what worked, what failed, and how I overcame challenges can be just as rewarding. This is supported by the authors' claiming that the purpose of prototyping is to communicate and explore an idea as effectively as possible. As a result, this reading made me reconsider the importance that prototyping, reminding me that experimentation and iteration are the fundamental aspects of design.
